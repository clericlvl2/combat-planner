# ADR-014 — App-level composition layer owns modal invariants

Status: **Accepted** (2026-07-26).

**Decision.** Call sites never assemble `Dialog`/`Drawer` directly. Every adaptive modal renders
through `src/lib/components/app/ResponsiveModal.svelte`, which owns the breakpoint split (one
`MediaQuery('(min-width: 1024px)')` — `Dialog` at ≥1024px, `Drawer` below), the scroll region
(present on both breakpoints), the sticky footer (rendered outside the scroll region, as a
sibling after it), `onSubmit` form wrapping, two size tokens (`form` | `compact` — no raw
`max-w-*` at a call site), fixed chrome (border/radius/title type), and the drawer-scoped touch
policy (`[data-responsive-modal='drawer']`). All six modals in the app —
`ConditionPicker`, `TagAssignmentDialog`, `CombatFormDialog`, `CombatantForm`,
`LibraryEntryFormDialog`, `NumpadSheet` — go through it.

**Rationale.** shadcn-svelte (ADR-008) is a copy-paste generator, not a library — it supplies
parts (`Dialog`, `Drawer`, their sub-parts), not assemblies. The assembly layer is ours to define,
or ours to re-decide at every call site. Before this ADR, six of six modals assembled their own
`Dialog`/`Drawer` pair and diverged on seven separate decisions (desktop width, mobile width,
desktop border, title type, mobile padding, height cap, sticky-footer behavior) — one of those
divergences (no height cap on `dialog-content.svelte`) was a latent off-viewport defect, and
another (missing sticky-footer construction) had already shipped as a defect. A single composition
component makes the divergent shapes unrepresentable rather than merely fixed one call site at a
time.

**Alternatives.** Leave each call site to assemble `Dialog`/`Drawer` by hand, documented in a
convention doc only (rejected — conventions this granular do not survive copy-paste under time
pressure; the divergence table above is what six of six looked like in practice). Fork
`dialog-content.svelte`/`drawer-content.svelte` per size instead of a wrapping component (rejected
— duplicates the breakpoint-split and touch-policy logic instead of centralizing it).

**Consequences.** A new modal is a new `ResponsiveModal` call site, never a new
`isDesktop ? Dialog : Drawer` pair. A genuinely new modal shape (that `ResponsiveModal`'s two size
tokens and fixed chrome cannot express) means extending `ResponsiveModal`, not forking it or
reaching for `Dialog`/`Drawer` directly. `ConfirmDialog` is deliberately out of scope — it uses
`alert-dialog`, which has different a11y semantics, and may be re-based on `ResponsiveModal` in a
later row. `touch-pan-y` moved off the base `ui/input` and `ui/textarea` components onto
`ResponsiveModal`'s drawer-scoped rule, so non-drawer inputs (desktop, and any future non-modal
surface) no longer carry a touch policy they don't need.
